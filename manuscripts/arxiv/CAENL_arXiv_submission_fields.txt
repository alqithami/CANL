Abstract (1596 characters; abridged from the paper for the metadata field):
Active learning couples acquisition to changing representations. CAENL separates acquisition, Dynamic Collapse Regularization (DCR), and coefficient control. Its supervised objective penalizes excess normalized within/between-class scatter with a separation safeguard. Analysis establishes invariances, conditional local descent, and coefficient bounds, without implying global stability or robustness. A 206-run ImageNet-100 study filters exact content before training, independently selects controls, tests ten paired seeds on two backbones, and holds acquired labels constant for component comparisons. At 60% annotations, DCR with MACC-Lite exceeds entropy alone by 1.900 and 1.612 percentage points on ResNet-50 and ResNet-18, surviving the prespecified 17-comparison Holm corrections. Common-label ratio-only regularization improves accuracy by 2.120 points; added feedback remains unresolved against tuned fixed control. A separate 160-model CIFAR-100 confirmation across four uniform label budgets establishes no adjusted AULC superiority over CE, full DCR, or a selected Fisher trace-ratio control. Ratio DCR costs 11.4% more recorded job time than CE, without an established AULC gain. Completed five-radius attacks on 30 classifiers establish no primary 2/255 robustness advantage. A separate 58-run raw-source AudioCaps study establishes no adjusted CIDEr-D gain. Earlier spectral studies yield a small C4 loss reduction and worse diffusion FID. The evidence supports the tested ImageNet-100 scatter intervention while bounding predictive, adaptive, robustness, and cross-task claims.
